Multi-site IT buildout

My day job. I took a four-site business's technology in-house and rebuilt most of it from the cabling up: networks, more than 50 cameras, access control and phones, including two sites where the answer was long-range wireless instead of a trench.

Role
Sole IT, hired to bring everything in-house
Sites
4, from a two-access-point office to a 25-camera property
Users
12 staff and about 900 tenants
Network
4 routers and 12 switches (MikroTik, UniFi, Netgear), with VLANs at every site
Cameras
More than 50 installed, 44 of them UniFi
Phones
10 Yealink VoIP phones
Long-range wireless
Gigabit bridges across spans of up to 500 meters, at two sites
Remote management
Tailscale

Network design, VLANs, Cat6 cabling, point-to-point and point-to-multipoint wireless, IP cameras, access control, VoIP, solar and battery power, Tailscale, vendor management, troubleshooting

The goal

A growing multi-site business had aging security and access control systems, and an outside IT provider that couldn't keep up. I was brought on to take everything possible in-house:

  • Replace the access control systems with a subscription-free option
  • Rewire and replace every security camera
  • Upgrade the old Cat5 cabling to Cat6
  • Expand the existing wireless networks and add new ones
  • Bring a newly acquired location up to the same standard: new cameras, new cabling and a new network, rack included

How it works

The four sites differ a lot in size, so each network is built to fit:

  • The smallest, an executive office: one router, one switch, two access points and a couple of cameras.
  • The largest: a router, six switches, 25 cameras, two access points, two VoIP phones, and a long-range wireless bridge with one base radio and two remote radios.
  • The second largest: a router, four switches, 15 cameras, a VoIP phone, and a wireless bridge with one base radio and one remote radio.
  • The fourth: a router, a switch, five access points, seven VoIP phones, a couple of cameras and several workstations.

Every site is segmented the same way: public Wi-Fi, cameras and phones each get their own VLAN, apart from the office computers. I manage the access control software at every site remotely over Tailscale.

Key decisions

  • Wireless instead of a trench. At the largest site, the original coax camera lines ran underground with no conduit in places, and a new $120,000 parking lot now sat between the buildings. Rather than cut into it, I linked three buildings with gigabit wireless bridges across spans of up to 500 meters. That replaced the coax cameras with IP cameras and extended the network across the whole property.
  • Solar and battery for the remote cameras. At the second site, cameras were needed hundreds of meters from power and from the wired network. A wireless bridge, solar panels, a battery and a switch that handles both solar charging and PoE cost less than extending power and Ethernet that far.
  • Bridges built to grow. I set up both bridges so they can run point-to-multipoint: another cluster of cameras needs one more remote radio, not a whole new link. At the first site I used that almost immediately, and added a second remote point.
  • UniFi, tested at home first. I trialed UniFi's ecosystem in my own rack before rolling it out at work.

Problems and fixes

  • A "faulty" access control panel that wasn't. After reports of a failing panel, I narrowed the fault to one wire that only worked some of the time. I traced it, found rodent damage and patched it. A replacement panel would have cost about $3,000, and it wouldn't have fixed anything.
  • A switch written off after lightning. The previous provider gave up on a PoE switch after a lightning strike left it with dead ports. I got every port working again, PoE included, and it now runs in my mini-lab.
  • A server years past retirement. One of my first jobs was retiring a Windows Server 2003 machine. It was long past its last security update, and a waste of power besides.

Results

  • Four sites on networks I designed and installed: 4 routers, 12 switches, 9 access points and VLANs throughout.
  • More than 50 cameras installed, 44 of them UniFi.
  • About 900 tenants on the Wi-Fi and access control, and 12 staff on 10 VoIP phones, with me as their support.
  • About $40,000 to $45,000 in equipment and services, planned and bought by me.
  • A newly acquired location brought up to the same standard. It's now one of the four sites, and setting it up was a large part of my last year.

What's next

  • Tailscale for everyone. I manage these systems over Tailscale today. Next I'd extend it to every staff device that needs remote access.
  • More camera clusters at the second site. Its bridge is ready for point-to-multipoint, so covering another remote corner of the property means adding a remote radio and a camera cluster.
